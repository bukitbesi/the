How to convert images

  1. Choose or drop images. Phone photos are turned upright using their EXIF orientation.
  2. Pick the output format. WebP for websites, JPG for photos that must open anywhere, PNG for screenshots and logos, ICO for favicons.
  3. Set quality or a target size, and resize if needed. Results update as you change settings.
  4. Download each image, or all of them as a ZIP.

Quality, size and resizing

  • Resize first. A 4000 px phone photo shown 1200 px wide wastes about 90% of its pixels; fitting it to 1200 px cuts the file far more than lowering quality.
  • Quality 75–85% looks the same as 100% for most photos at a fraction of the size. Below about 60%, blocks and smudges appear around edges and text.
  • Target file size finds the highest quality that fits a limit, useful for forms that reject files over 100 KB or 1 MB.
  • PNG, ICO and BMP are lossless: quality does not apply, and only resizing makes them smaller.

Converting does not restore quality. A JPG turned into PNG keeps its JPEG artefacts and just gets bigger. Keep your originals, and convert from the best copy you have.

Metadata and privacy

Converting redraws the pixels, so camera details, dates and GPS location are left behind unless you tick Keep metadata. To clean photos without re-encoding them, use the EXIF Remover; to add copyright that Google Images shows, use the EXIF Editor.
