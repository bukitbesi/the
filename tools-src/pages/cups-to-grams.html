Example: a chocolate cake with 2 cups of flour

The recipe asks for 2 cups flour, 1 cup sugar, ½ cup oil, 1 cup milk, ¼ cup cocoa, 2 eggs and 1 tsp salt. In a 250 ml cup that is 254 g flour, 209 g sugar, 115 g oil, 258 g milk, 22 g cocoa, 120 to 130 g of egg and 6 g salt. Baking it in a 9-inch round tin instead of an 8-inch one? Multiply everything by 1.27.

How the conversion works

  1. Volume: cups × the cup size (250, 240 or 236.6 ml), tablespoons × 15 ml, teaspoons × 5 ml.
  2. Density: grams per US cup from King Arthur Baking and USDA, divided by 236.6 ml, gives grams per ml.
  3. Weight: volume × grams per ml. Where the two sources differ, both are shown as a range and the first (spooned and levelled) is used.
  4. Recipes and tins: every line is multiplied by the serving factor and the tin area ratio (round: π × d² ÷ 4; square: side²).

Tips for accurate measuring

  • Flour: spoon it into the cup and level with a knife. Scooping from the bag packs it up to 20% heavier.
  • Brown sugar: press it firmly into the cup; recipes assume packed.
  • A kitchen scale is the most accurate for cakes and bread. Use this chart for recipes that only give cups.
  • Not in the chart: cake flour, rice flour, glutinous rice flour, gula melaka and fresh grated coconut, because published figures conflict. Weigh these.

Sources

  • King Arthur Baking: Ingredient weight chart and USDA FoodData Central
  • US FDA: household measures (240 ml cup, 15 ml tablespoon, 5 ml teaspoon)
  • KPDN: egg grades and weights (P.U.(A) 223/2022)

Disclaimer: Weights are estimates for spooned and levelled ingredients. Brand, humidity and how you measure can change the real weight by 5 to 15%; use a scale for the most accurate result.
