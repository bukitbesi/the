How the comparison is worked out

  1. Up-front cost of buying = down payment + transfer and loan stamp duty + SRO 2023 legal fees + SST.
  2. Each year: the buyer pays the instalment and ownership costs; the renter pays rent. Whoever spends less invests the difference.
  3. The renter invests the buyer's up-front cost in year one.
  4. At the end: buyer = home value × (1 − selling costs) − loan balance + investments; renter = investment pot.

When renting makes more sense

  • You may change job or city within 3 to 5 years.
  • Prices in the area are flat or oversupplied.
  • You reliably invest the difference (for example ASB, voluntary EPF or an index fund).

When buying makes more sense

  • You will stay a long time and rents in the area are high relative to prices.
  • You qualify for the first-home stamp duty exemption or schemes such as the Housing Credit Guarantee Scheme.
  • A fixed instalment gives certainty against rent that rises every year.
