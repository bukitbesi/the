Malaysian IC Number Decoder

A MyKad number reads YYMMDD-PB-###G: the first six digits are the date of birth, the 7th and 8th are the place-of-birth code (for example 14 is Kuala Lumpur, 10 is Selangor), and the last four are a serial. Type a number to see the birth date, age and state or country of birth, using JPN's official code lists.

Codes for people born outside Malaysia

{{C_ABROAD}}
CodeCountry or region

JPN's country list has a few inconsistencies (for example Byelorussia appears under 64 though Belarus is 92), so codes 83 to 93 are shown as regions rather than single countries.

Example

900101-14-5679: 90-01-01 is 1 January 1990, a Monday; 14 is WP Kuala Lumpur; 5679 is the serial, and its last digit 9 is odd, which by convention means male. As of {{C_VERIFIED}} this person is 36.

How the decoder reads a number

  1. Clean: dashes and spaces are removed; exactly 12 digits are needed.
  2. Date: YYMMDD must be a real date (29 February only in leap years). If both 19YY and 20YY have passed, the most recent is chosen and a note appears.
  3. Place: the 7th and 8th digits are looked up in JPN's Kod Negeri and Kod Negara lists; unlisted codes are flagged.
  4. Last digit: odd or even is shown with the digit itself, because the gender convention is not published by JPN.

Privacy and your MyKad

  • A MyKad number is personal data under the Personal Data Protection Act 2010. This tool never sends, stores or puts it in the page address.
  • Only authorised officers (JPN, police, customs, on-duty armed forces and authorised public officers) may inspect a MyKad (National Registration Regulations 1990, regulation 7(1)).
  • For a list, the CSV export keeps only the masked number (for example 900101-14-****).
  • Need an exact age for a form? Use the Age Calculator.
