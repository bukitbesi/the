The section 18A formula

Section 18A was added by the Employment (Amendment) Act 2022 and has applied since 1 January 2023. When a monthly-rated employee does not complete a whole month because they:

  • start work after the first day of the month,
  • leave before the last day of the month,
  • take unpaid leave, or
  • are called up for national service,

the pay for that month is monthly wages ÷ days in the wage period × days eligible. The days are calendar days: 28, 29, 30 or 31.

Worked examples

  • New joiner: RM3,000, starts 16 October 2026: RM3,000 ÷ 31 × 16 = {{P_EX_JOIN}}.
  • Resignation: RM4,500, last day 10 September: RM4,500 ÷ 30 × 10 = {{P_EX_LEAVE}}.
  • Unpaid leave: RM6,200, two days in August: deduction RM6,200 ÷ 31 × 2 = {{P_EX_UNPAID}}, pay {{P_EX_UNPAID_PAY}}.

Daily rate by month length

The same salary gives a different daily rate in a 28, 30 or 31-day month. The 26-day rate is the ordinary rate of pay used for overtime, rest-day and public holiday work.

Calendar days, working days or 26 days?

  • Calendar days (section 18A): the statutory formula. Rest days and public holidays inside the period are paid.
  • Working days: counts only work days. It pays the same as calendar days on average, but can differ by month depending on how many weekends fall in the period.
  • 26 days: the ordinary rate of pay under section 60I, meant for overtime and rest-day pay. Applied to a part month it often underpays a new joiner.

Employees in Sabah and Sarawak are covered by the Sabah and Sarawak Labour Ordinances, which have their own rules.
