What a Malaysian invoice should show

  • Every invoice: a unique serial number, the date, your business name and address, the customer, a description, quantity and price of each item, and the total.
  • SST-registered businesses (Service Tax Regulations 2018, regulation 10): your SST registration number, any discount, the amount excluding tax, the tax rate and tax amount shown separately, and the total including tax. Foreign-currency amounts must also be shown in ringgit.
  • Sales tax (goods): the buyer's name and address, and the type, quantity and amount of each item (Sales Tax Regulations 2018, regulation 7).
  • Language: Bahasa Melayu or English; this generator does both, including the amount in words.

Rounding to the nearest 5 sen

Bill totalRounded (cash)

Bank Negara Malaysia's rounding mechanism (since 1 April 2008) rounds the total bill, never each item, for payments made at the counter. Published guidance differs on whether card payments are included, so tick the box only if you round totals at your counter; leave it off for bank transfers.

How the totals are worked out

  1. Each line: quantity × unit price, less the line discount, in whole sen so nothing drifts.
  2. Whole-bill discount: spread across the lines in proportion before tax, so the tax is charged on what the customer really pays.
  3. Tax: each line's rate (service tax 8% or 6%, sales tax 10% or 5%) is applied and rounded to the sen; totals are shown per rate.
  4. Total: subtotal − discount + tax + delivery, optionally rounded to the nearest 5 sen, then less any deposit to give the balance due.

Invoice or e-invoice?

An invoice made here is a normal invoice (PDF or paper). An LHDN e-invoice is different: it is submitted to the MyInvois system and validated with a unique ID and QR code. Since 1 September 2026, businesses with annual turnover below RM3 million are exempt from e-invoicing, unless a corporate shareholder, holding company, related company or joint venture has turnover of RM3 million or more. Exempt businesses keep issuing normal invoices like these. Check your own start date with the e-Invoice Checker.

Tips for getting paid faster

  • Clear terms: state the due date (14 or 30 days) and your bank account or DuitNow details in the notes.
  • One number per document: use New document to move to the next number; never reuse a number.
  • Quotation first: send a quotation, then switch the same details to Invoice once the customer agrees.
  • Deposit: enter what the customer has already paid and the invoice shows the balance due.
