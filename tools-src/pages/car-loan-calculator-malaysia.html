New hire purchase rules from 1 June 2026

The Hire-Purchase (Amendment) Act took effect on 1 June 2026. Banks have until 31 March 2027 to update their systems.

  • Reducing balance: interest is charged only on what you still owe, so it falls as you repay.
  • EIR disclosure: offers must show the effective interest rate, which is directly comparable between banks.
  • No more Rule of 78: settling early returns all the interest you have not yet been charged.
  • Existing loans: flat-rate agreements signed earlier keep their terms; banks are offering goodwill discounts to settle them early.

Flat rate to EIR conversion

The EIR is the reducing-balance rate that gives the same instalment as a flat rate. Because a flat rate charges interest on the full loan for the whole term, the EIR is close to double.

How flat-rate car loans and the Rule of 78 work

For agreements still on a flat rate: interest = loan × rate × years and instalment = (loan + interest) ÷ months. RM90,000 at 2.50% flat for 9 years costs {{K_FLAT_PAY}} a month and carries {{K_FLAT_INTEREST}} interest, equal to {{K_FLAT_EIR}} EIR.

When you settle early, the Rule of 78 rebate is total interest × k(k + 1) ÷ n(n + 1), where k is the months left and n the total months. Settling that loan after 4 years costs {{K_R78_PAY}}, saving {{K_R78_SAVED}} of the remaining instalments but {{K_R78_EXTRA}} more than reducing balance would.
