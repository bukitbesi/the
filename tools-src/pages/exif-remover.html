How to remove EXIF data from photos

  1. Choose or drop your photos. Each one is read on your device and listed with what it revealed: GPS location, date, camera, serial numbers, owner name, software, thumbnail and more.
  2. Check the options. The defaults keep orientation and the colour profile, which hold no personal data and stop photos from turning sideways or changing colour.
  3. Download. Each clean copy is named -clean. With several photos, Download all saves one ZIP.
  4. Check a result by opening it in the EXIF editor: "Found in this photo" should say no personal metadata.

What it keeps, and why

  • Image data, byte for byte. Nothing is decoded or recompressed, so there is no quality loss and no change to the pixels.
  • Orientation (optional, on). Without it, phone photos stored sideways display sideways. The remover writes a new, minimal EXIF block holding only the orientation.
  • Colour profile (optional, on). Wide-gamut photos need it to show correct colours. It contains no personal data.
  • Image structure: the JPEG JFIF and Adobe colour-transform markers, PNG transparency, gamma, physical size and animation chunks, and WebP alpha and animation chunks.
  • Copyright credit (optional, off). Tick it to keep Creator, Credit Line, Copyright Notice and licence links in a fresh XMP block, so Google Images can still credit you while GPS and camera data go.

Removing location on your phone or computer

This page cleans any photo in seconds, but your device can also stop leaks at the source:

  • iPhone: when sharing, tap Options at the top of the share sheet and turn off Location. To stop recording it, go to Settings, Privacy and Security, Location Services, Camera and choose Never.
  • Android (Google Camera): open the camera settings and turn off Save location. Other brands use a similar "location tags" switch.
  • Windows: right-click the file, Properties, Details, Remove Properties and Personal Information.
  • Mac: in Preview, Tools, Show Inspector, the info tab, GPS, Remove Location Info.

Disclaimer: the remover deletes the metadata it recognises (listed above). It cannot remove what is visible in the picture itself, such as street signs, faces or reflections, and a site you upload to may still record when and from where you uploaded.
